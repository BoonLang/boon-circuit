# Review of the Boon compiler rewrite plan

Reviewed: `docs/plans/BOON_COMPILER_REWRITE_PLAN.md` at a60a11d6 (branch
`compiler-rewrite-plan`), with its notes as input. Written 2026-09-29/30 by an
independent review session that had not taken part in writing the plan. Status:
**review input for the owner; not authority.** Nothing here changes a decision;
proposals are marked as such.

## How this review was made

- **Lenses.** Five review lenses from the owner's brief (evidence, language
  semantics, architecture and performance, migration and execution, what is
  missing), each worked by independent finder agents that read the plan, the
  notes, today's code and the original Boon documentation, and ran probes on
  today's compiler (`target/release/boon_cli` built from a60a11d6) where a
  claim could be tested.
- **Measurement.** Every headline number was re-measured (`measurements/`), the
  breakage census was re-counted with a scanner over all 177 tracked Boon files
  and the Boon strings in Rust tests (`census/`), and five prior-art topics were
  researched with primary sources (`research/`).
- **Adversarial verification.** The finders' 163 raw findings were merged into
  152 by topic, then every one was handed to a refuter whose job was to refute
  it against the plan text, the code, a probe or the original documentation.
  A finding is **confirmed** when the refuter could not refute it,
  **dropped** when it could. Refuters also corrected severities and wording;
  the tables below show the severity **after** refutation, and the finding
  text incorporates their corrections. Dropped findings are listed at the end
  with the reason.
- **What was not done.** The two batches of prior-art research that were cut
  by the usage limit (incremental compilation; state restore) and the host-runner
  observation of today's effect re-run behaviour are listed under "Not covered"
  and stay open.

Deliverables in this directory:

| --- | --- |
| `REVIEW.md` | this document: verdict, findings by severity with evidence and proposed plan changes |
| `PLAN_EDITS.md` | the proposed plan text, section by section, ready to apply once the owner approves |

## 1. Executive summary and verdict

**Verdict: go with changes.** The strategy is right and the diagnosis is
sound, but the plan is not yet executable as written: its language section
(§4.3) has gaps that make the first milestone (S1, the counter) either an error
or a blank screen under a literal reading, its runtime changes are a scheduler
rewrite disguised as a delta, its switch is a big bang, and its estimates omit
the largest items. None of that argues against the rewrite. It argues for a
second, short decision round with the owner and a re-baselined plan before P0
exits.

**What holds.** Every headline number reproduces on a fresh build (§2 below):
the baselines within 10%, the 30x call-site amplification exactly, the
SHA-256/CBOR shares within one point, the warm path equal to cold, the
prototype parser within 6%. The old compiler is a weak oracle, as claimed
(the denominators are wrong, the conclusion is not). The direction of the
architecture (per-function schemes instantiated once per call site, no
self-proofs, a flat single-pass front end, lowering straight to a plan) is
supported by the code reading and by the prior-art notes. The greenfield
decision D1 is not questioned by any confirmed finding.

**What does not hold, ranked by consequence.**

1. **The change model is not yet a specification** (SEM-007, SEM-001,
   SEM-004, SEM-008, EVI-005, MIS-003). The plan never says which reads
   *fire* (re-run the reader) and which *copy* (sample a committed value), and
   never says what happens at activation. Read literally, a WHEN over a value
   with a start value has no value until its selector updates, so every view in
   the examples renders nothing at start (or, for a constant selector, is a
   "never runs" error); the cycle rule rejects the canonical counter in one
   sentence and admits an unbounded loop through a HOLD's piped input in
   another; the tie rule's "same step" is undefined; and the D12 clause that
   decides what persists cannot be evaluated. Every one of these was
   reproduced with probes on today's runtime. They are all instances of one
   missing table (fire vs copy edge per construct) plus one missing rule
   (activation). The proposed text is in `PLAN_EDITS.md` §4.3.
2. **Three owner decisions need a second answer**, not because they are wrong
   but because two readings are possible and the readings lead to different
   compilers: what a copy context does at activation (D30/D31/D32), whether
   reactions to same-tick writes run per microstep or once per tick in rank
   order (D21/R8; the microstep reading has a glitch that reaches commands),
   and what "exactly the fields the function consumes" means through
   forwarding and shared records (D23; the literal reading has no principal
   scheme and breaks passing a row to a view function). `QUESTIONS.md` puts
   each with snippets, options and a recommendation.
3. **WHEN/WHILE under D30 is the event/value split in disguise** (SEM-012,
   SEM-011). The measured census finds about 335 WHENs whose arms read a value
   that updates independently of the selector; 36 of 37 hand-checked must
   become WHILE. The plan's own Theme draft copies `mode` when `name` updates,
   so its light/dark toggle would stop working. A hover hint will not carry
   this migration; the review proposes an error with a fix-it and asks the
   owner to confirm.
4. **R7 + R8 rewrite the executor's scheduler** (ARC-001, ARC-002, ARC-008,
   MIS-002). Today's executor has no commit phase and dedups equal HOLD writes
   in about 18 places; copy ops, microsteps, every-write-fires and D33 resets
   replace its dispatch. No spike sizes this, R8/R9 hide inside P4's estimate,
   and the "plan semantics version" for old plans is unspecified. The review
   proposes a runtime track R-CHG with S5 widened to "scheduler v2".
5. **P7 is a big bang** (MIG-020, EVI-007). The old-engine compatibility rule
   keeps every next-only migration (List/replace_all, Http/get and send, host
   ports, Cells) off main until the switch, while P3b, P4 and P5 exits need
   them. A per-example engine flip is proposed.
6. **Targets are estimates presented as gates** (ARC-020, ARC-011, ARC-023,
   ARC-013). Only the front end is measured; the check target is 4–5x below the
   notes' own model; the ≤8 ms warm goal and ≤1 ms no-op gate contradict the
   plan's own 10.7 ms full-recheck sum; the ≤40 ms edit-to-preview budget
   covers the compile only. S2 must become a real measurement gate with a
   re-baseline band, and the §6 table needs a provenance column.
7. **Oracles and process** (MIG-025, MIG-024, MIG-028, ARC-019). O1 has no
   author, size or independence rule and would be written from a spec that
   predates D14–D36; the change rules have no oracle independent of the new
   compiler; budgets v4's prose and draft disagree on every limit; error
   recovery, the thing an editor lives on, is one sentence.
8. **Effort** (MIG-023, MIG-021, MIG-017). P0 holds six spikes and a spec
   rewrite in "2 weeks"; the migration is about 1,050 edits (56% in NovyWave);
   P7 is not "days". The reviewer's range is 24–34 weeks of critical path and
   50–70 engineer-weeks, against the plan's 20–27 and 32–45.

**Decisions flagged as unsound or in conflict** (none is overturned here;
each is an owner question): D25 speaks of WHEN arms where only WHILE arms can
hold state (L6); D9 forbids collections in HOLD while D15 lets a LATEST hold
them and D12 persists it; D31 says queries restart when arguments "change"
while D32 abolishes the notion of change; D35 "no reachability rule" while §4.5
keeps one; D12 lists LATEST as durable while §4.5 does not; D16 describes a
Cells design that no rule in the plan lets a developer write (the smallest
fix is counted iteration with `Stream/pulses`, which the foundations plan
already sanctions).

**What the review did not do.** Two prior-art topics (incremental compilation,
state restore) and the host-runner observation of today's effect re-run
behaviour were cut by the usage limit; they are listed in §8 as open.
Findings were verified by one refuter each (two for critical ones), not by a
panel; 9 of 152 were dropped on refutation and the rest carry their refuter's
corrections.

**Recommended next step.** One owner session on the seven questions in
`QUESTIONS.md` §A; then apply `PLAN_EDITS.md`; then start P0 with the edge
table, activation rule and step rule as its first deliverable, S2 and the
scheduler spike as its first measurements, and the census in `census/` as the
migration baseline.

### SEM-007 [critical] WHEN over a constant or start-valued selector has no defined start evaluation; view WHENs render nothing and constant-selector WHEN reads as an error

*Decisions: D25, D30, D31, D32; Plan: §4.3 Change rules (lines 380-393), D25 (line 94), R8 (line 613); Sources: S1, S5; verdict: confirmed*

§4.3 says a copy context (THEN body, WHEN arm) 'runs once each time the input updates', D32/§4.3 say start and activation are not updates, R8 says copy ops 'evaluate only when the input updates', and §4.3:386-387 says 'THEN or WHEN over something that never updates is an error'. Read literally: (a) `filter |> WHEN { All => 32 … }` where `filter` is bound to a literal at the call site (TodoMVC filter_button, theme_name_button, every `Theme/material(of: Surface)` dispatcher) is a 'never runs' error; (b) a WHEN over a HOLD (`mode |> WHEN { Light => …, Dark => … }`, `todo.completed |> WHEN {…}`) has no value until the selector's first later update, so the document renders nothing (L3c) at start. The plan elsewhere assumes WHEN results have a start value (D30 lists THEN outputs but not WHEN outputs among 'first value later'; the stale-copy hint bullet speaks of 'a WHEN over a value with a start value'; D25 presupposes WHEN over compile-time constants is legal) but never states when the arm is first evaluated. If activation evaluation is added, it must also say that a command (D31) inside such an arm does not run at activation.

### MIG-001 [high] D16 Cells cannot be written as the plan's row describes; the smallest fix is a pure List/fold (or accepting K unrolled passes)

*Decisions: D9, D16, D21, D32, D33; Plan: §5.1 Cells row (line 750); §4.3 lines 367, 384-387, 405-407, 418-421; D9, D16, D21, D33; R6; Sources: M2; verdict: confirmed; severity critical -> high after refutation*

The §5.1 row says computed values live in HOLD or collection state updated on edit events, recalculation follows dependency order, and a visited/depth guard marks cycles. Under the plan's own rules none of that can be written. (1) A per-cell HOLD updated on the commit event reads the committed snapshot of other cells (D21). So after A1 changes, B1 = A1+1 lags one edit behind. (2) A second pass would need a re-trigger, but D33 says a HOLD's own writes do not re-trigger it. §4.3:405-407 makes 'a THEN input, WHEN selector or update candidate on the cycle' an error. ### SEM-035 [high] D23 exact record parameters are ill-defined for forwarding/shared records and break passing whole rows to view functions

*Decisions: D13, D20, D23, D28; Plan: §1 D23; §4.3 Contracts (lines 445-450); §5.1 'Exact record parameters'; Sources: S2, S5; verdict: confirmed*

D23 says a record passed to a user FUNCTION must contain *exactly* the fields the function consumes, where consumes = reads or forwards into an exact position (callee parameter, state, collection, contract). Worked through the DAG this rule is not well-defined for pass-through functions and produces contradictions for shared records: (1) `h(r) = f(r: r) + r.b` with `f(r) = r.a + 0` — h consumes {a} (via f) ∪ {b}, so r must be exactly {a, b}, but the inner call `f(r: r)` then passes the extra field b to f, whose exact set is {a}: a hard error, so every shared record must be projected at every call (`f(r: [a: r.a])`), and every later change to f's reads ripples into every caller's projection. (2) `id(r) = r` and `keep(r) = [color: Red, ...r]` (the TodoMVC Theme idiom) consume nothing directly, so under the literal rule every call with a non-empty record is an 'extra fields' error; the only sensible exact set for such parameters is the *demand of the consumers of the result*, which is known per call site, not per definition. (3) `save(r) = rows |> List/append(item: r)`: the exact set is whatever the collection's readers project anywhere in the program (consumer-directed, whole-program). (4) A value passed to two callees with exact {a} and {a, b} (`[f(r: v), k(r: v)]` at the root) cannot satisfy both. (6) Tagged-object payloads (D20 elements, `HierarchyPage[...]` with 10 fields) — D23 speaks only of records; if payloads were exact, `label_of(el) = el |> WHEN { Button[label] => label … }` could never receive a real Button. ## 8. Not covered, and how the review itself could be wrong

- **Not observed:** today's effect re-run behaviour under a host runner (does
  a THEN-triggered transient effect re-run when a HOLD read by its intent
  changes; does an arm-scoped effect fire at startup). The plan's notes read
  this from `machine.rs`; the review's observation agent was cut by the usage
  limit. R9's "commands staged once" is a behaviour change only if today
  re-stages, so this stays a P0 item (`PLAN_EDITS.md`, S6).
- **Research not done:** incremental compilation at sub-frame latency
  (rust-analyzer/salsa, Zig, Lean, tsgo) and state restore (Android, VS Code,
  Automerge). The findings that touch those areas (ARC-009 to ARC-011,
  ARC-019, SEM-028 to SEM-033, MIS-007) rest on the code and the plan alone.
- **Verification depth:** one refuter per finding (a second for critical
  ones), reading the plan, the code and the original documents and re-running
  probes; not a panel. Nine findings were dropped. Refuters lowered the
  severity of about a third of the rest; the tables show the lowered values.
- **Census precision:** the WHEN→WHILE count (about 335) is a token-level
  scanner with a hand-classified sample of 40; the real number is the new
  checker's. The D23 count (93–140) is an upper bound from the same scanner.
- **Measurement noise:** the machine was shared (load 1–4); medians of 10
  fresh processes were used, not the budgets' 3+30 protocol.
- **What a finder could not see:** owner intent that lives outside the plan
  text. Where the plan's wording admits two readings, the review says so and
  asks, rather than assuming the intended one.
