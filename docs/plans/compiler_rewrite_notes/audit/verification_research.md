## key
verification_research
## topic
Self-verification, proofs, digests and determinism kept off the hot path: what production compilers do, and where Boon's product compile path pays for proving its own output (measured with CPU-time sampling of the current release binary)
## findings
- system: Boon (this repo, measured today; method note) | technique: Baseline attribution. samply is unusable because /proc/sys/kernel/perf_event_paranoid=2, and changing it is a system security setting. I used a gdb CPU-time sampler instead: ITIMER_PROF plus SIGPROF, backtrace per sample, fresh-process verified and diagnostics runs of target/release/boon_cli. As a sanity check, the per-phase sample ratios match unperturbed phase timers within about 5% on TodoMVC at 1 ms (check:finish 1.29 sampled vs 1.33 real) and about 12% on NovyWave at 4 ms, where memory-bound phases are slightly over-weighted. Scripts and raw samples are in <session scratchpad, not kept>/verif/ (prof.py, attrib.py, union2.py, sha_callers.py, *_samples.json). | numbers: TodoMVC verified (2,941 compile samples): 21.7% of samples have SHA-256 on the stack (about 170 ms of about 790). Frames tagged receipt/digest/proof/verify/validate: 10% of parse+typecheck (about 46 ms) and 58% of semantic through seal (about 200 ms), so about 30% of the whole compile. Inside the semantic phase alone it is 51%. NovyWave verified (1,714 samples over 3 runs): SHA-256 26% of samples (about 400-490 ms of 1,870), canonical CBOR+SHA (boon_contract::canonical_serde_hash*) 15%, CBOR encoding 5%. Tagged work is 30% of parse+typecheck and 46.5% of semantic through seal, about 39% overall and 52% of the semantic phase. That reproduces, and slightly exceeds, the plan's '45% of semantic' claim. Diagnostics: SHA-256 is 3.4% of samples on TodoMVC and 11.7% on NovyWave. Local SHA-256 speed with no SHA-NI on this i7-9700K: 582 MB/s for 16 KB blocks and 173 MB/s for 64-byte messages (about 370 ns per small hash), measured with openssl speed. | relevance_to_boon: The largest single lever in this area is not the '49 checks'. It is hashing: SHA-256 over self-describing serializations, run inside one process, on data that no consumer reads. | source: Local measurement: gdb/ITIMER_PROF sampler over ./target/release/boon_cli compiler-sample ... --mode fresh-process; openssl speed sha256; BOON_KERNEL_TRACE / BOON_SEMANTIC_TRACE / BOON_COMPILER_LOWER_TRACE stderr traces
- system: LLVM / Clang | technique: The IR Verifier runs in debug and assertions builds, in tests (opt -verify-each, lit), and on explicit request (-fverify-intermediate-code, LTO verify-each). Release clang passes -disable-llvm-verifier to cc1 by default. LLVM_ENABLE_ASSERTIONS defaults ON only for Debug builds, and LLVM_ENABLE_EXPENSIVE_CHECKS defaults OFF. Correctness assurance comes from the test suite, fuzzing (OSS-Fuzz, Csmith/YARPGen) and Alive2 run offline, not from the shipped compiler re-checking itself. | numbers: Verifier cost is described as 'a few percent' of build time when on. LLVM docs: assertions 'typically slow down LLVM and Clang'. Expensive checks are 'time/memory expensive', default OFF. | relevance_to_boon: verify_plan, boon_ir verify_static_schedule/verify_hidden_identity, semantic validate_freshly_constructed and the receipt parity checks are Verifier-class checks. They belong under cfg(debug_assertions) or an 'expensive-checks' cargo feature that tests and CI enable, not in the release preview path. Boon has no such lane: zero cfg(debug_assertions) in boon_plan, boon_semantic, boon_checked and boon_parser. | source: https://llvm.org/docs/CMake.html ; https://clangbuiltlinux.github.io/CBL-meetup-2020-slides/nick/debugging_llvm.html (clang -### shows -disable-llvm-verifier) ; https://reviews.llvm.org/D153468 ; https://clang.llvm.org/docs/ClangCommandLineReference.html
- system: MLIR / CIRCT firtool (Chisel's FIRRTL compiler) | technique: The MLIR PassManager verifies after every pass by default, and firtool keeps that default (cl::opt verifyPasses("verify-each"), cl::init(true), pm.enableVerifier(verifyPasses)). Verifiers are local, per-op structural invariant checks: no hashing, no re-deriving global results, O(ops). Users switch them off with --verify-each=false. Industrial users gate them off in release: ROCm's MLIR stack disabled verification on its hot compile paths behind NDEBUG. | numbers: ROCm PR #485: the verifier cost about 17 ms per config, and removing redundant verification made compilation about 11% faster. | relevance_to_boon: This is the only mainstream counterexample of verify-on by default, and it survives only because its checks are cheap and local. Boon's in-product checks are the opposite: whole-structure re-serialization plus SHA-256, and re-derived digests compared at crate handoffs. The 11% ROCm figure matches Boon's plan_validation share (90 of 790 ms, 11.4% on TodoMVC). | source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/llvm/circt/main/tools/firtool/firtool.cpp ; https://mlir.llvm.org/doxygen/PassManager_8h_source.html ; https://www.jeremykun.com/2023/09/13/mlir-verifiers/ ; https://github.com/ROCm/rocmlirTriton/pull/485 ; https://circt.llvm.org/docs/Dialects/FIRRTL/RationaleFIRRTL/
- system: rustc (MIR validation, incremental fingerprints, verify-ich) | technique: (1) MIR is validated after every pass only with -Zvalidate-mir, a fuzzing/CI flag. It is validated always exactly once, at the phase change to Runtime(Optimized): one boundary check instead of per-pass re-checks. (2) Incremental fingerprints use a 128-bit stable non-cryptographic hash (SipHasher128 via StableHasher). Interning uses FxHasher. The dev guide says 'computing fingerprints is quite costly' and is 'the main reason why incremental compilation can be slower than non-incremental compilation'. Queries whose results never need a fingerprint are marked no_hash, and non-incremental builds run with a disabled dep graph and track or hash no results. (3) -Zincremental-verify-ich re-hashes cached results. It is on by default only as a 1/32 sample of queries loaded from disk. | numbers: verify-ich on every query cost up to about 50% in the worst case, so it is sampled at 1/32: at most 7% regression (coercions incr-unchanged) and typically under 0.5%. | relevance_to_boon: (a) A fresh-process Boon compile has no incremental consumer, yet it builds kernel currentness receipts (11 ms TodoMVC, 44 ms NovyWave via BOON_KERNEL_TRACE receipts_us), per-definition SHA-256 basis fingerprints (owner.rs:1053, about 4% of NovyWave diagnostics samples), the dependency manifest V7 (100 ms NovyWave) and a request graph that only tests read. rustc would not compute any of these in a non-incremental build. (b) Where fingerprints are needed, rustc uses a 128-bit non-crypto hash, not SHA-256. (c) If paranoia requires an in-product check, sample it (1/32) or move it off the latency path. | source: https://doc.rust-lang.org/nightly/nightly-rustc/src/rustc_mir_transform/pass_manager.rs.html ; https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/queries/incremental-compilation-in-detail.html ; https://github.com/rust-lang/rustc-stable-hash ; https://doc.rust-lang.org/beta/nightly-rustc/rustc_data_structures/fx/struct.FxHasher.html ; https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/pull/90361 ; https://blog.rust-lang.org/2021/05/10/Rust-1.52.1/
- system: GHC (Core Lint, STG/Cmm lint) | technique: -dcore-lint / -dstg-lint / -dcmm-lint / -dlint are documented under 'Debugging the compiler'. GHC's user guide describes them as heavyweight intra-pass sanity checks of the compiler itself, not of user code. They are off by default and enabled in GHC's validate and testsuite configurations. Clash, which compiles Haskell to hardware through GHC, inherits these defaults (inference, not separately verified). | numbers: Described as 'heavyweight'. No product-build cost is paid because they are off. | relevance_to_boon: Boon's receipts, execution-expression 'proofs', checked-image pairing receipts and semantic image validations check Boon's own sanity, not the user's. They are Core-Lint-class and belong in a lint lane that CI turns on for every example. | source: https://ghc.gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/doc/users_guide/debugging.html ; https://github.com/ghc/ghc/blob/master/validate
- system: CompCert (verified compiler) and translation validation | technique: Correctness proofs are checked once, when the compiler is built, in Coq. The shipped compiler does not re-prove anything. A few passes (register allocation) use a posteriori translation validation at compile time. That validator is itself proved correct, targets exactly one pass, and runs a linear dataflow check. | numbers: The Rideau & Leroy (CC 2010) regalloc validator adds about 20% to register-allocation time and about 6% to total compile time. Its soundness proof is about 900 lines of Coq. | relevance_to_boon: In-product validation earns its cost only when (1) it is part of a correctness argument (a proved checker), (2) it is scoped to one transformation, and (3) it is linear and allocation-light. Boon's receipts and digest joins meet none of these: they are unproved re-derivations whose value is testing, which CI provides at zero product cost. If Boon later wants proof value (for example WHERE contracts), use CompCert's 'verified validator' shape: a small, independent, linear checker. | source: https://xavierleroy.org/publi/validation-regalloc.pdf ; http://gallium.inria.fr/blog/register-allocation/ ; https://compcert.org/
- system: Alive2 / Csmith / YARPGen (offline oracles) | technique: Bounded translation validation (SMT) and random-program differential testing run over test suites and fuzz corpora in research and CI. They are never inside the production compiler. | numbers: Alive2 over LLVM's unit tests: 47 new bugs, 28 fixed. Csmith: 481 bugs reported (about 120 miscompilations) in GCC, LLVM, CompCert and others by 2013. YARPGen: 220+ bugs in GCC, LLVM and ICC. | relevance_to_boon: Boon's strongest correctness oracle would be differential execution: run the plan against a reference interpreter or flat-oracle plan on scenario traces, over generated Boon programs. That runs in CI and costs the product lane nothing. The repo already has a feature-gated test-flat-oracle (boon_compiler/src/lib.rs:785-800). That is the right shape; the in-product proofs are the wrong shape. | source: https://users.cs.utah.edu/~regehr/alive2-pldi21.pdf ; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3453483.3454030 ; https://users.cs.utah.edu/~regehr/yarpgen-oopsla20.pdf ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220752409_Finding_and_Understanding_Bugs_in_C_Compilers
- system: Cranelift / regalloc2 / Wasmtime | technique: regalloc2's symbolic checker (abstract interpretation over allocations) is driven by fuzz targets, not by production compilation. Cranelift's enable_verifier exists, but Wasmtime's Config::cranelift_debug_verifier defaults to false and is described as 'largely only useful for the developers of wasmtime itself'. | numbers: cranelift_debug_verifier default = false. | relevance_to_boon: This is a direct template for boon_plan::verify_plan and boon_ir's verifiers: fuzz or CI oracle, off in the product. | source: https://cfallin.org/blog/2022/06/09/cranelift-regalloc2/ ; https://github.com/bytecodealliance/regalloc2 ; https://docs.wasmtime.dev/api/wasmtime/struct.Config.html
- system: WebAssembly engines (V8 Liftoff, Wasmtime) | technique: Validation happens once, at the trust boundary where untrusted bytes enter. V8 fuses decode, validate and baseline codegen into one streaming pass. Wasmtime's Module::new decodes and validates. Module::deserialize of Wasmtime's own precompiled output is unsafe and 'only lightly validated': trust comes from provenance (engine/config compatibility), not from re-validating. | numbers: Liftoff makes one pass over opcodes, validating while generating code. | relevance_to_boon: verify_plan is Wasm validation. It belongs in decode_program_artifact / MachineTemplate::new, where serialized or untrusted plans enter, not in handoffs between producer and consumer in the same process. SealedMachinePlan already exists as a typestate token, and MachineTemplate::new_sealed already trusts it (boon_plan_executor/src/machine.rs:9524-9527). But its only constructor runs verify_plan (boon_plan/src/lib.rs:10036-10037), and boon_program_runtime then re-runs verify_plan on the sealed plan anyway (program_host.rs:498). | source: https://v8.dev/blog/liftoff ; https://chromium.googlesource.com/v8/v8/+/HEAD/docs/wasm/architecture.md ; https://docs.wasmtime.dev/api/wasmtime/struct.Module.html
- system: Proof-carrying code (Necula 1997) | technique: The producer ships code plus a certificate. The consumer runs a cheap checker at install/load time. The whole point is that checking happens across a trust boundary and is much cheaper than proving. | numbers: n/a (design principle) | relevance_to_boon: Boon builds 'receipts' and 'seals' in the producer and re-checks them in a consumer inside the same address space and binary: checked_image_publication.rs:687-690 recomputes two whole-image digests at the typecheck-to-semantic handoff. With no trust boundary, this buys nothing that a Rust typestate token (private constructor) doesn't give for free. | source: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/263699.263712 ; https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~necula/Papers/marktoberdorf.pdf
- system: Bazel / Nix (reproducible builds, content addressing) | technique: Cryptographic digests are computed once, over final bytes, at the artifact/CAS boundary, and cached (Bazel caches file digests per file metadata). Bazel's default digest is SHA-256; BLAKE3 has been selectable since 6.4 because it is 'significantly faster' on large inputs, and flipping the default was proposed for Bazel 8. Nix hashes the NAR serialization with SHA-256 once, when a path enters the store, and truncates to 160 bits for store paths. | numbers: BLAKE3 is about 7-8 GB/s single-thread with AVX2 per the BLAKE3 paper and secondary benchmarks, against 0.58 GB/s SHA-256 measured on this machine: more than 10x. | relevance_to_boon: Crypto hashes belong only where bytes leave the process (app package bundle digest, content store, persistence, distributed wire). Compute them once over the one canonical byte string that gets stored. Boon instead SHA-256s the plan binary in seal, SHA-256s it again in the runtime's verify_plan, CBOR-encodes the plan and SHA-256s those bytes twice (ContentArtifact::new, then validate_content_artifact). | source: https://github.com/bazelbuild/bazel/discussions/22011 ; https://bazel.build/reference/command-line-reference ; https://nixos.org/guides/nix-pills/18-nix-store-paths.html ; https://nix.dev/manual/nix/2.22/protocols/store-path ; https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~cs4601/refs/papers/blake3.pdf
- system: Salsa / rust-analyzer (reactive incremental engine) | technique: In-memory reuse uses revision counters, recorded dependency edges, Eq-based backdating (early cutoff) and durability levels. It needs no cryptographic hashing of results; hashing is needed only for on-disk persistence. | numbers: Durability lets a revision that changes only low-durability inputs skip validating queries over high-durability inputs entirely. | relevance_to_boon: Boon's planned warm path (T5) should key reuse on revisions plus Eq, with at most 64/128-bit fast fingerprints for persisted caches. It should not reuse the SHA-256 receipts and 'V14/V17/V18' fingerprints that cold compiles currently pay for. | source: https://salsa-rs.github.io/salsa/reference/algorithm.html ; https://rust-analyzer.github.io/blog/2023/07/24/durable-incrementality.html ; https://docs.rs/salsa/latest/salsa/struct.Durability.html
- system: Elm (reactive-UI compiler) | technique: Speed comes from per-module interface (.elmi) and object (.elmo) artifacts cached in elm-stuff, plus doing little work per module. There are no global proofs or receipts on the compile path. | numbers: 0.19.2 performance work reported up to 1.9x (351 modules: 4.98 s to 2.60 s). | relevance_to_boon: The fast reactive-language compilers have no self-proof layer at all. Boon's user-facing guarantees (types, WHERE contracts via boon_verify::verify_explicit_contracts, which costs about 0.05 ms) are language semantics and stay. Everything that proves Boon's own pipeline agrees with itself can move to CI. | source: https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/elm-1-0-roadmap-faster-builds ; https://blog.poleprediction.com/posts/elm-compilation-elmi-files/

## recommendations_for_boon
- Split into three explicit lanes and make the default lane fast. PRODUCT (diagnostics, verified preview, playground): user-program semantics only, meaning types, OUT, WHERE contracts (boon_verify::verify_explicit_contracts, 0.05 ms, keep) and capability limits. No self-verification, no receipts, no crypto hashing. DEBUG (cfg(debug_assertions); the test suite already builds this way): all structural verifiers. CI / cargo feature `expensive-checks`: verify_plan after the backend, boon_ir verifiers, semantic image and receipt construction plus parity, digest recomputation at handoffs, and differential oracles. Run the CI lane on every example and on generated programs.
- Fix T4.2 in docs/plans/BOON_COMPILER_PROFILE_DRIVEN_PLAN_2026_09_28.md before anyone implements it. It proposes a trusted seal that 'computes plan_sha256 and skips the 41-check verify_plan'. Sampling shows plan_sha256 is 96% (TodoMVC) and 85% (NovyWave) of verify_plan, so that change saves about 4 ms of 90 on TodoMVC. The trusted seal must skip the hash too: make SealedMachinePlan carry a lazily computed digest (OnceLock) that only package build, persistence and hot-reload identity consumers force.
- Give SealedMachinePlan two constructors: a crate-private `seal_trusted(plan)` used by machine_plan_backend, zero cost, typestate only; and `seal_untrusted` (= verify_plan) used only by decode_program_artifact and MachineTemplate::new. This is the Wasmtime Module::new vs Module::deserialize split. Delete the second verify_plan in boon_program_runtime (program_host.rs:498 via artifact_from_compiled, program_core.rs:694-712) and keep only the cheap capability-limit checks.
- Encode once, hash once, store those bytes. Replace boon_plan::binary's self-describing encoder (it writes the struct name and every field key per instance, binary.rs:224-228 and 345-350) with a schema-versioned positional encoding using varints and sorted maps. The same buffer should serve the artifact payload (instead of a separate ciborium pass, program_core.rs:359), persistence and the digest. Use BLAKE3 at artifact boundaries (more than 10x SHA-256 on this CPU with no SHA-NI); keep SHA-256 only where an external format mandates it. Fix ContentArtifact::new, which hashes and then immediately re-hashes in validate_content_artifact (boon_persistence/src/lib.rs:775, 779 and 2904): hash on construction, verify only on load.
- Stop using SHA-256 for anything that never leaves the process. Interning and hash-consing (PlanRowExpressionArena::intern, boon_plan/src/lib.rs:8991, which does serialize-then-SHA per node, dedup hits included) should use FxHash/foldhash over structural fields plus Eq. Cross-edit reuse keys (kernel basis fingerprints, owner.rs:1053 via the StableSha256Hasher bridge at receipt.rs:3153-3238) should use a 128-bit non-crypto stable hash, as rustc does (SipHasher128), or xxh3-128. Remove the Hash-trait-to-Vec-copy-to-SHA bridge entirely.
- Do not build incremental metadata for requests that cannot use it, as rustc does with its dep graph disabled when not incremental. In fresh-process and diagnostics requests, skip kernel currentness receipts (owner.rs:4662-4667; 11/44 ms), the semantic dependency manifest V7 and request graph (100 ms NovyWave; session.rs:804-809 retains it with only test readers), ExecutionReceiptPublisherV5 and per-expression execution 'proofs' (semantic_image.rs:1471-1500 and 4845; about 44 ms TodoMVC and about 170 ms NovyWave sampled), and the construction-image digests. When T5 adds warm sessions, build reuse state Salsa-style (revisions, recorded edges, Eq backdating), not by re-hashing whole artifacts.
- Delete in-process digest joins at crate handoffs. For example, CheckedImageKernelPairingReceiptV1::__typechecker_new recomputes entity-route and ownership digests of an image the same process just sealed (boon_checked/src/checked_image_publication.rs:687-690). Also delete the duplicate boon_ir verify_static_schedule/verify_hidden_identity pair (boon_ir/src/lib.rs:322-323, then again at boon_compiler/src/lib.rs:1412-1413). A private-constructor token already proves provenance, and erase_and_lower_bundle's own comment (boon_ir/src/lib.rs:224-227) already states this principle.
- Move determinism guarantees into CI, where they are cheap and stronger. Compile every example twice, in two processes, with different thread counts or hash seeds, and compare the canonical plan bytes; the test harness does the byte or hash comparison, not the compiler. Replace 'same plan_sha256' as the main regression gate with a behavioural differential oracle (execute plan vs flat-oracle or reference interpreter on scenario traces, extending the existing test-flat-oracle feature) plus canonical-bytes equality. Version the canonical format independently of Rust type and field names, so representation refactors are not blocked by digest churn.
- If some in-product assurance is politically required, copy rustc's verify-ich compromise. Run structural verify_plan checks (not the hash) on a 1/32 sample of compiles, or on a background thread after the preview is published, off the latency path, and report failures as internal compiler errors.
- Amend the documents and lints that currently block this. docs/plans/BOON_COMPILER_PERFORMANCE_PLAN.md:139-140 ('do not authorize ... skipped verification') must distinguish checking the user's program (keep) from re-verifying compiler output (debug/CI lane). crates/xtask/src/architecture.rs:1405-1431 makes SealedSemanticImageV5, semantic_program_digest and verification_manifest_digest mandatory direct fields of product types. Make them feature-gated or a sidecar, or the lint will reject the fix. The AGENTS.md measurement protocol (A/B/A 3+30 in two lanes and two cold modes) should allow a lighter gate for deletions of proof machinery that leave user-visible output unchanged.
- Shrink what is verified and hashed. The TodoMVC plan's document table has 64,482 expression rows (29,917 of them 'constant' rows over only 439 constants), about 10.7k unique after hash-consing (heuristic estimate). Hash-cons document expressions in the backend and move compiler_id provenance to the debug_map sidecar. Every remaining verify, serialize and hash pass scales linearly with this table.
- Harness hygiene (not scored, but in every developer's loop): compiler_sample.rs:909 SHA-256s the 39 MB boon_cli binary on every invocation (about 70 ms); cache it by path+mtime+inode as Bazel does. compiler_sample.rs:1863-1866 pretty-prints a 20.4 MB JSON plan just to hash it (about 106 ms); hash the compact canonical bytes instead.
## anti_patterns_seen_in_boon
- crates/boon_plan/src/lib.rs:11497 - verify_plan ends with `plan_hash: plan_sha256(plan)?` (lib.rs:14184-14189: encode the whole plan with boon_plan::binary, then SHA-256). Sampled at 96% (TodoMVC) and 85% (NovyWave) of verify_plan time. The 49 check IDs are the cheap part.
- crates/boon_plan/src/binary.rs:224-228 and 345-350 - the 'canonical' encoder writes the Rust struct name (serialize_str(name)) and every field key string with a u64 length prefix for every struct instance. This inflates hashed bytes (TodoMVC estimated 23-40 MB against 9.17 MB compact JSON) and couples plan_sha256 to Rust identifiers.
- crates/boon_plan/src/lib.rs:10036-10037 - seal_shared_machine_plan is the only way to obtain a SealedMachinePlan and always runs the full verify_plan, even for plans the backend just built in-process (called from boon_compiler/src/lib.rs:535).
